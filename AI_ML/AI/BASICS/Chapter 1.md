# 8. Birth of AI

The idea became official in **1956**, when researchers gathered at the Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence.

One of the organizers, John McCarthy, coined the term **"Artificial Intelligence."**

Their bold idea was:

> Every aspect of learning or intelligence could, in principle, be described precisely enough that a machine could simulate it.

That workshop is widely regarded as the birth of AI as an academic field.

# 10. Components of AI

Modern AI systems are built from several important pieces:

* **Data** – the information AI learns from.
* **Algorithms** – the methods used to learn from data.
* **Models** – the trained systems that make predictions.
* **Computing power** – CPUs, GPUs, and specialized AI chips that perform massive calculations.
* **Feedback** – information that helps improve the model over time.

Without good data, even the best algorithm usually performs poorly.

# 11. Types of AI

AI is commonly grouped into three levels based on capability:

1. **Narrow AI (Weak AI)** – designed for one specific task.
2. **General AI (AGI)** – a hypothetical AI that could perform intellectual tasks across many domains like a human.
3. **Super AI** – a theoretical AI that would surpass human intelligence in almost every field.

Today, almost every AI product you use belongs to the first category.

---

# 12. Narrow AI

Examples include:

* Spam filters
* Voice assistants
* Language translators
* Recommendation systems
* Chess-playing programs

A chess AI can defeat world champions but cannot cook dinner or write software unless it has been built for those tasks.

---

# 13. General AI (AGI)

AGI would be able to:

* Learn new skills without being redesigned.
* Transfer knowledge between unrelated tasks.
* Reason broadly across many domains.

This remains a research goal rather than an achieved technology.

---

# 14. Super AI

This is a theoretical concept describing AI that would outperform humans across virtually all intellectual activities.

Whether it will ever exist—and how it should be governed—is an active topic of research and debate.

# 15. Common AI Myths

Some common misconceptions are:

* **"AI knows everything."** No—it depends on its training and available information.
* **"AI is always correct."** It can make mistakes or produce incorrect information.
* **"AI is conscious."** Current AI does not have feelings, desires, or self-awareness.
* **"AI understands like humans."** It processes patterns and representations, not human experience.

# 18. Why AI Became Popular Recently

Although AI has existed for decades, several factors accelerated its progress:

* Vast amounts of digital data became available.
* Powerful GPUs made large-scale training practical.
* Better algorithms were developed.
* Cloud computing reduced infrastructure costs.
* Advances in deep learning and large language models dramatically improved performance.

The combination of these advances—not a single breakthrough—is what made today's AI possible.

---

# 19. Limitations of AI

Current AI still has important limitations:

* It can inherit biases from its training data.
* It may generate convincing but incorrect answers.
* It lacks human emotions and genuine understanding.
* It depends on data quality.
* It can struggle with situations that differ greatly from its training.

Understanding these limits is just as important as understanding AI's strengths.
